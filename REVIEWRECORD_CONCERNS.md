# Review Record — public concern disposition list

Manual tracking. There is no automated audit log, and nothing here is a formal
review. Comments and issues are informal input. No independent cryptographic
review has happened.

Statuses: **adopted**, **under examination**, **unresolved**, **declined**.
Every entry gets a reason. Declined and unresolved entries stay listed.
Here, **finding evidence** is distinct from **disposition** (how the concern is handled)
and **mitigation** (whether a remedy has actually been selected and implemented).
A synthetic regression reproducing a limitation does not mean the mitigation is adopted
or that the protocol is security-qualified. Credit: handle, name, or none, as
the reporter chooses.

### Clarification of the bounded probe (2026-11-02)

[demonstrated here] The 84 byte/removal mutations use the **mocked-anchor exit-0 control**, not the separate offline exit-2 control. **All 84 return exactly exit 1**; per-case path, operation, exit, counts and non-pass codes are retained in [the revised JSON](https://github.com/GnomeMan4201/review-capsule/blob/d3145c67a4bbf79195c4d5c97d4f9a13afcfb07e/reviewrecord/evidence/external-concerns-2026-11-02/meta-probe.json). Anchor proof byte checking in this sweep is performed by the test double, not a real qualified anchor adapter. The 64 cases are all subsets of the six supported statuses (including empty), testing only the exit fold.

E-3's offline exit 2 is **not fork detection**. With explicitly mocked qualified anchors, the full log and omitted-C alternate both reach exit 0. The attested-withdrawal and disabled-disposition examples also reach exit 0 under the mock; these expose the scope of current semantics, not a demonstrated signature bypass or real-round qualification. Exhaustive per-code coverage remains **[unknown]**. No meta-test-pass or fork-detection claim is made. Mitigation remains unresolved.
